The best prompts I’ve seen came from lawyers describing a task they hate, not from engineers.

3

What you’ve tried, good or bad.

Especially the bad. A tool that fooled a careful lawyer is more useful to the room than one that worked.

Resources and sources

Where to go next.

Ethics

The two controlling opinions

  • N.C. State Bar, 2024 Formal Ethics Opinion 1, “Use of Artificial Intelligence in a Law Practice” (Nov. 1, 2024). Six inquiries with answers: competence, vendor diligence, in-house tools, signing pleadings, client disclosure, billing.
  • ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 29, 2024). Maps the existing Model Rules onto generative AI; creates no new obligations.
  • Neither body wrote an AI-specific rule. Rules 1.1, 1.6(c), 3.1, 3.3, 5.3 and N.C. R. Civ. P. 11 stayed where they were.
The EDNC matters

Read the orders yourself

Behind the demo

The thesis

  • Cuffney, Ethically Implementing Generative AI in Prosecution (Northwestern University, 2025). Six career prosecutors evaluated AI summaries across ten redacted public-records cases; their criticisms became the rules in the prosecutor-analysis skill.
  • Statute and instruction libraries built from ncleg.gov and the NC School of Government’s pattern jury instructions.
